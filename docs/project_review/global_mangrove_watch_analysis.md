# Global Mangrove Watch (GMW): fair comparator

**Owner and users.** Global Mangrove Watch is a Global Mangrove Alliance platform used for conservation, policy and restoration planning. The Global Mangrove Alliance’s *Global Mangrove Watch 4.1 Launch*, **27 July 2026**, [source](https://www.mangrovealliance.org/news/global-mangrove-watch-4-launch) describes annual mangrove extent **1985–2025** and **monthly loss alerts** through June 2026. Wetlands International’s *GMW 4.1 leaflet*, **2026**, [source](https://www.wetlands.org/publication/global-mangrove-watch-leaflet/) explains platform products. JAXA’s *GMW 4.1 dataset* page, **2026**, [source](https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ALOS/en/dataset/gmw_e.htm) confirms annual epochs. The [public map](https://www.globalmangrovewatch.org/) shows extent, change, disturbance alerts and other layers (Global Mangrove Watch; live platform, accessed 12 Nov 2026).

**Method and coverage.** GMW is global and explicitly maps *extent/change* with annual time series and disturbance alerts. The version 4.1 extent record describes Landsat, Sentinel-2 and JAXA L-band SAR; XGBoost baseline classifications, 130 Earth Engine Random Forest scene classifiers, Bayesian updating/Markov filtering and visual QA. It reports **global extent F1 0.9276** from 32,569 reference points across 120 sites, with local accuracy expected to vary. These are extent-map metrics, **not alert accuracy in the Sundarbans**. Source: Bunting et al., *Global Mangrove Watch: Timeseries of Mangrove Extent v4.1.12*, **13 July 2026**, [dataset record](https://zenodo.org/records/21346457). GMW also provides area subscriptions and customizable AOIs; this directly weakens any Lens claim that local alerts or notifications are unique (Wetlands International/Global Mangrove Alliance, *GMW 4.1 Launch*, **2026**, [source](https://www.mangrovealliance.org/news/global-mangrove-watch-4-launch)).

**What Lens can and cannot claim.** GMW already offers the capabilities most likely to be mistaken for a gap: maps, historical change and near-real-time alerts. Lens currently adds an **AOI monthly NDVI expected-vs-observed trace, co-located SAR/CO context, an explicit review policy and generated evidence report** in a prototype workflow; whether this is useful beyond GMW is untested. Lens does **not** currently have a validated 10–30 m loss map, restoration success assessment, a larger data archive or a proven user advantage over GMW. GMW alerts and Lens anomalies target different variables, so compare them on a common incident set before using a better/worse claim.

**Documented practical limits, without inventing failure.** An ESA-funded Radar Alerts for Mangrove Monitoring project reported that an earlier GMW Sentinel-2 alert workflow could be delayed by clouds and water-level false positives in some regions; its *2025* report described a typical three-month confirmation lag in affected areas. Its experimental Sentinel-1 radar complement had low commission but poor timeliness: about 50% of tested detections appeared within 395 days of the reference change. These are **specific earlier workflows and sites**, not a measured limitation of GMW 4.1 in the Sundarbans. Source: Dean, Smith, Bunting and Hilarides/Hatfield Consultants for ESA, *RAMM Final Report*, **13 June 2025**, [official report](https://eo4society.esa.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ESA11697_FINAL-REPORT_20250613_v1.0.pdf). The current Lens pipeline has the same tide/cloud risks and no event-level timing study. A fair trial should blind reviewers to platform, use independent field/high-resolution imagery and compare alert precision, lead time, coverage, review minutes and actionability.
